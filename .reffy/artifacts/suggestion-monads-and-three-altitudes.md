# Suggestion for the Next Draft: Leibniz, the Monad, and Three Altitudes

For the editors of *Durable Forms*. A suggestion for Draft 0.36, written
against **Draft 0.35** (November 2026). Section references are to 0.35.

Date: 2026-11-17. Source thread: `logical-assembly-modes-and-monads.md`
(project `paseo-core`, workspace `nuveris-v1`), which also draws on
`lasm-understanding-the-stack.md` (project `nuveris`). Like the mode thread
in 0.35, this suggestion is kept high level, with no stack implementation
named.

## Summary
1. **Draft 0.35 already holds most of the earlier thread.** Spinoza and the
   mode (Chapter 1, Chapter 8 §III), Kant's wall named and declined
   (Chapter 1), and Wittgenstein's hinges with "no frame draws itself"
   (Chapter 10 §I, §IV). Nothing here revises those.
2. **What's new is Leibniz.** The mode names the loop's unit; nothing yet
   names the *participant*, the party that reads and acts through the
   assembly. Leibniz's monad fits it closely, and his harmony fits the
   assembly itself.
3. **Three altitudes would let the book hold the Spinoza–Kant question
   without settling it.** One order at the level of the record, many points
   of view at the level of the participants, and the conditions of the frame
   at the level of the people who draw it. Each tradition speaks for the
   altitude where it is strongest.

## 1. Leibniz, between Spinoza and Kant
Chapter 1 moves from Spinoza (one substance, its modes) to Kant (the wall)
and declines to settle the question between them. There is a figure
in between, historically and conceptually. Leibniz visited Spinoza in The
Hague in 1676 and read parts of the *Ethics* in manuscript. The
*Monadology* (1714) is in large part his answer: not one substance but
infinitely many simple substances, **monads**, each a point of view on the
whole universe.

Three of Leibniz's claims carry over:
- **Monads are windowless** (*Monadology* §7). No monad acts causally on
  another.
- **Each mirrors the whole from its own point of view** (§56). No two
  mirrors are alike.
- **They agree anyway, through a pre-established harmony** (§78), not
  through interaction.

**Suggested placement:** a short paragraph in Chapter 1, between the Spinoza
paragraph and the Kant paragraph, planting the monad and the harmony for
Chapter 8. It also completes the relay's logic: Leibniz reacted to Spinoza,
and Kant's critical turn reacted largely against the Leibniz–Wolff school.

## 2. The monad as the participant, the assembly as the harmony
Chapter 8 §III names the mode as "the one thing" each turn of the loop
produces. It doesn't name who takes part. In the applied system that
participant is a **use case**: a purpose that records what happens, writes
it, gives it meaning, derives state from it and acts on it. The applied
work places any use case with five questions:

1. What happens that's worth recording?
2. Who records it?
3. What does it mean?
4. What's derived from it?
5. Who acts on it, and where?

Read against Leibniz:
- **Answering the five questions is stating the monad's complete
  concept.** Once stated, a participant can join without knowing any other
  participant.
- **The assembly is the harmony.** Participants agree not because they call
  one another but because each reads the same declared meaning. That is the
  book's narrow waist, seen from the side of the parties it serves.
- **Each participant's derived state is its mirror:** the same record seen
  from one point of view.
- **The identity of indiscernibles gives a practical test:** two
  participants that answer all five questions the same way are one
  participant.

**The correction the book already knows how to make.** Leibniz's harmony is
*pre-established*, guaranteed by God. That is Plato's flaw over again, a
form assumed to hold by its own nature. The book's correction applies
directly: the assembly's harmony isn't given in advance, it is kept, held by
enforcement and tested at the gates. Chapter 1 already says the assembly's
authority is "positional and earned, not essential and given". The harmony
is the same.

**Suggested placement:** Chapter 8 §III, after the mode is named: "if the
mode is the loop's unit, the monad is its participant", with the five
questions in prose and the harmony correction. Possibly a sentence in
Chapter 7 where the assembly's consumers are discussed.

**A tension to state rather than hide:** Chapter 1 keeps a hierarchy, with
the assembly prior to its projections, and Chapter 9 calls projections
"derived, plural, disposable". Monads are substances, not derivations. The
two fit only if the monad is the *participant* (the use case and its five
answers, which persist) and not the *projection* (an application generated
to serve it, which may be disposable). The draft should keep those two
words apart.

## 3. Three altitudes
Chapter 1 frames the open question as "whether meaning and the world are
one order seen twice, or two orders that meet only at a limit". A way to
hold it without settling it is to give each answer its own altitude:

| Altitude | Tradition | What it holds | Where it is in 0.35 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **The record** | Spinoza | one order, under two descriptions; the mode as its unit | Ch. 1; Ch. 8 §III |
| **The participants** | Leibniz | many points of view coordinated by a kept harmony | not yet present |
| **The frame** | Kant, the later Wittgenstein, Langer | the conditions under which anything can appear in the assembly at all, and the judgment that draws and amends them | Ch. 10 §I (hinges), §IV ("no frame draws itself") |

Two points make the third row more than a label:
- **The five questions sit at this altitude.** They are not one more
  participant. They are the form any participant must take to appear, which
  is close to what Kant means by conditions of possible experience.
- **The judgment that fills them sits here too.** Chapter 10 §IV already
  says assigning meaning to the residue "is the frame-drawing act, and no
  frame draws itself". Kant's *Critique of Judgment* names that faculty:
  bringing particulars under concepts. The person who rules on what an act
  was (the second mask of Chapter 8 §III) is exercising it.

This keeps the book's honesty about the Spinoza–Kant question. It doesn't
claim the two are reconciled. It claims the assembly needs both, at
different heights: an immanent record below, and a frame drawn from outside
above. Langer and Wittgenstein's place in the book becomes that upper
altitude, rather than a counterweight to Spinoza.

**Suggested placement:** the closing paragraph of Chapter 1's Kant passage
could forecast the three altitudes, and Chapter 10 §VI ("What Is Kept")
could close on them.

## Cautions
- **Rivals, used as levels.** Spinoza and Leibniz disagreed about exactly
  this, one substance or many. The suggestion uses each at a different
  altitude and should say so, so a careful reader doesn't think the book
  missed the dispute.
- **The evidence is still self-referential.** The two use cases the
  applied work has run (governing the system's own runtime, and calibrating
  its witness) both *build the assembly*. A participant that looks outward
  hasn't been placed yet. Until one has, the five questions are a proposal,
  not a demonstrated form, and the draft should present them that way.
- **"Use case" is the applied work's word.** The book may want its own:
  *participant*, *party* or simply *monad*.
