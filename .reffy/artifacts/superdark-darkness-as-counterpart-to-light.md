# Superdark: Darkness as a Counterpart to the New Source of Light

*Idea log, Tuesday, November 24, 2026*

Source: Poliks, Alonso Trillo, Dunn, Scott-Douglas and Springett, *The
Superdark Factory*, Chapter I, "Darkness" (Antikythera). Full text in
`superdark_factory.md`; only Chapter I has been read for this note. Related:
`logical-assembly-a-new-source-of-light.md`,
`suggestion-monads-and-three-altitudes.md`. Written against Draft 0.36.

The pairing with the light figure is mine. The authors never mention
lithography. They do reach for light once, and in the book's sense: in the
simplest factory a mystery "can be resolved by focusing a light on it."

## What the chapter argues

**Darkness.** As a task is automated it retreats inside the machine, and the
firsthand knowledge of whoever used to supply it withdraws. "Darkness is the
failure of descriptive information to be *useful*." It is neither secrecy nor
illegibility: information can be "disclosed and readable but not useful to an
observer attempting to predict, audit, coordinate, or steer." It carries no
negative connotation.

**Three classes.** Class 1 automates execution, Class 2 plans, Class 3
objectives. A snapshot explains a Class 1 factory if you hold the plan. A full
log explains a Class 2 factory if you hold the objective. In Class 3 the
objective is produced inside, from the world, so "there is no key with which
to decode the factory's self-description." That limit is superdarkness.

**The neurotic governor.** Someone who will supply no input until they could
reconstitute the factory by hand. The chapter prices this: logs cost more and
explain less the faster the factory runs, until the control tower shrinks to
one button, kill.

**Radiant darkness.** Darkness is also generative. Readable space is what can
be traced back to what the governor supplied; the best readable solution can
never outscore the best solution. AlphaGo's Move 37 sits at the frontier of
readable space. AlphaFold sits outside it, fed by the world. Thompson's
evolved circuit sits outside it too, and is useless: it works on one piece of
silicon and cannot be reproduced.

**Moving in the dark.** Only the interior is dark. The factory is still
steered, by reading "the perturbations of the world in the wake of the
factory." It is dark even to itself, so its identity must be given from
outside: a line drawn by an architect who commits, once and irrevocably, never
to cross it.

## Where it meets Durable Forms

**1. The mask is the key.** On their account a record stays useful exactly as
long as the objective is supplied from outside. In the book's figure that is
the mask: human decisions shaping what the light prints. Take the mask away
and the same record, still fully readable, goes dark. This is the strongest
independent argument I have seen for why the mask, not the light, is the
book's subject.

**2. Light on the wake, not the interior.** The book's light reads acts and
records and says what they were. That is reading the wake. Even the superdark
factory needs this organ; the authors call evaluation its "primary sensory
organ." Light and darkness here are less opposites than a division of labor:
darkness inside, light on what comes out.

**3. "No frame draws itself," conceded.** The factory writes its own
objectives but cannot originate its own identity. Someone outside draws the
line. That is Chapter 10's claim arriving from the other direction. The
disagreement is over what follows: their architect draws once and withdraws,
and the charter is then cowritten with the factory; the book's people keep
deciding.

**4. Durability is their problem too.** They want configurations that "hold
durably to the norms" of whoever set them, and their cautionary case is a
pattern that cannot be reproduced on another wafer. Lithography exists to
print the same pattern everywhere. A durable form is one that has not
overfit.

**5. Windowless.** A factory "open to nothing, including itself, apart from
the traces it leaves within the world" is close to the monad of the Leibniz
thread, with the wake standing in for harmony.

## What it puts under pressure

- **Is the assembly a neurotic governor's tower?** The book needs a clear no:
  it records judgments and decisions, the surface where input is supplied, not
  a mirror of execution kept for reconstruction.
- **The cost of legibility.** score(*c\**) ≤ score(*b\**): confining a system
  to what its people can read only ever costs. The book should say what is
  bought at that price.
- **Remainder humanism.** They treat human and agent as interchangeable, and a
  human claiming the loop as a demotion of the factory. The book's case for
  people at the mask cannot rest on what machines supposedly cannot do. It has
  to rest on the frame needing an outside, which they grant.
- **Objectives are thinner than norms.** "Any objective specific enough to be
  scored is thinner... than the norm it represents." A code book has the same
  gap.

## Open questions

- Is the assembly a deliberately held Class 2, and is that a ceiling or a
  choice? Their answer: a choice that one competitor declining to make ends
  for everyone.
- Does darkness belong in the preface as the condition the light answers, or
  later, as a rival value the book answers directly?
- Does the three-altitudes scheme place this: darkness tolerable among
  participants, declined at the level of the frame?
- Chapter II's charter and versioning are the nearest thing to the mask and
  the record. Worth reading next.
