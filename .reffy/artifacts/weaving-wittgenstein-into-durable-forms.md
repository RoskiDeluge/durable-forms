## The one idea worth importing

The source note does two jobs. The first is a probe programme for latent space
and a design object, the *binding*, which belong to the rig and to Lasm's
engineering. The second is a philosophical reading that fits the book so well it
can pass for part of the book's own argument that went unsaid:

> **A LogicalAssembly is Tractarian in what it represents and late-Wittgensteinian
> in what keeps it true.**

That sentence is *Durable Forms*' thesis restated in Wittgenstein's vocabulary.
Chapter 1 keeps Plato's architecture and amends his physics. Wittgenstein went
through the same amendment himself. The *Tractatus* built a total, fixed logical
space whose objects are "unalterable and subsistent" (TLP 2.0271), which is
Plato's serene physics again, stated as logic. The *Investigations* then located
meaning in use and made a rule's content depend on a practice (PI §43, §202).
The book narrates its inheritance as a relay of corrections, each thinker
repairing the one before. Wittgenstein is the only figure in that tradition who
ran the relay inside a single career. Janik and Toulmin's line that the
*Tractatus* was finally "a kind of Platonic myth" makes the connection to
Chapter 1 almost for free.

The rest of this note is about placing that idea so it strengthens the existing
argument without turning the book toward latent space.

## Ground rules for the import

These come from the source note's own cautions and from the book's
Shannon-inspired discipline ("claims sized to their tests").

1. **Import the philosophy, not the rig.** No patchrig numbers, no vindex label
   counts, nothing from the Nuveris whitepaper, which is confidential. The
   source note says plainly that nothing has been measured and `FINDINGS.md` is
   empty. The book can state predictions. It cannot cite results.
2. **Keep flagged analogies flagged, or leave them out.** The note marks
   several resonances as only analogies: saying versus showing as the
   interpretability wall, 6.43 and directives, ethics taught by example. The
   book has no apparatus for "this is only an analogy". Where such a move can't
   carry its own hedge in one clause, cut it.
3. **Take the non-Kripkean reading of rule-following.** A practice gives a rule
   its content. It does not make whatever the practice approves correct. The
   book needs this reading, and suggestion 4 shows where one of its current
   sentences leans the other way.
4. **Don't let Wittgenstein displace Langer.** Langer carries the book's
   philosophical weight on symbols and on the formalization boundary, and she
   has the last word in Chapter 10. Wittgenstein should arrive as the source
   that Langer's "logical form" came from and as a correction of the Tractatus,
   not as a new protagonist.
5. **The essay genre holds.** Chapter 9 §IV refuses to specify a format, and
   the same refusal covers bindings and probes. One paragraph can name the
   binding. The programme stays in the rig.

## Suggestions, in priority order

### 1. Put Wittgenstein into the relay — Chapter 1 *(high)*

**Where.** [docs/01-inherited-forms.md](../../docs/01-inherited-forms.md). The
best spot is a short new section between §I (Plato and Aristotle) and §II
(Langer), which also keeps the chapter chronological (1921/1953 before 1942 is
fine, since Langer is placed as Wittgenstein's reader). A lighter option is two
or three paragraphs opening §II. The chapter intro's list ("Plato supplies the
architecture; Aristotle moves form into function; …") gains a clause either way.

**Move.** Wittgenstein appears twice, as the one thinker who corrected himself:

- *The Tractatus as the most rigorous Platonism ever built.* Logical space is
  total and fixed. Any proposition gives "the whole of logical space" (3.42),
  all possible states of affairs are given with the objects (2.0124), and the
  objects are unalterable (2.0271). The template governs the instance, and
  nothing maintains the template, which is Chapter 1's exact diagnosis of the
  Forms: "perfect, and connected to nothing that would fail if it were wrong."
- *The Investigations as the correction.* The meaning of a word is, "for a large
  class of cases", its use (PI §43). "'Obeying a rule' is a practice" (§202).
  Form moves from a fixed space into an activity with training, correction and
  checks, which is Aristotle's move from separate form to function carried into
  language.

**Draft.**

> Wittgenstein ran the whole relay alone. The *Tractatus* (1921) gave logic its
> own theory of Forms — a logical space fixed in advance, total, its objects
> "unalterable and subsistent" — and, like Plato's, connected to nothing that
> could fail if it were wrong. The *Investigations* (1953) took it apart from
> the inside: meaning is use; a rule is a practice; to think one is following a
> rule is not to follow it. He called the earlier book, in effect, a Platonic
> myth. This essay keeps the *Tractatus*'s ambition — a possibility space made
> explicit — and takes its physics from the *Investigations*.

**Why it helps.** It gives Chapter 1 a figure who shows that the architecture
and the physics come apart, since one mind held both and kept only one. It also
supplies Langer's missing antecedent (suggestion 2) and sets up the
rule-following argument that Chapter 5 can use (suggestion 3).

**Risk.** The section runs long. Two paragraphs are enough. Leave out the
picture theory's internals.

**Ripple edits.** Ch1 intro sentence, Ch1 §VI ("What Remains"), Ch10 §V (where
"the relay of Chapter 1 arrives"), the Ch1 description in
`docs/index.md` and its front matter, and Works Cited.

### 2. Name the debt under Langer's "logical form," and revisit the Preface — Chapter 1 §II, Preface *(high)*

**Where.** Ch1 §II: "she held that what a symbol shares with what it symbolizes
is precisely *logical form*". Also the Preface paragraph on "Logical", which
currently sets the philosophical lineage aside: "closer to this book's subject
than the philosophical one."

**Move.** Langer's "logical form" is borrowed from the *Tractatus*: a picture
depicts because it shares form with what it pictures (TLP 2.18). *Philosophy in
a New Key* engages Wittgenstein directly, and her discursive/presentational
distinction is in part a refusal of the Tractarian verdict that what cannot be
said must be passed over in silence. *Check the exact passages in PNK (ch. 3–4)
before asserting this in print.* Once that debt is named, the Preface's
dismissal of the philosophical lineage reads oddly, because the book's central
philosophical source is a logician of symbolic form writing in Wittgenstein's
wake.

**Options for the Preface.** (a) Keep ANSI/SPARC as the primary lineage and add
one sentence admitting the philosophical one: "Logical" in the Tractarian sense,
the shared form in virtue of which a representation can represent at all. (b)
Leave the Preface alone and let Chapter 1 carry it. Option (a) is better,
because the Preface's whole genre is to explain how the name contained the
argument, and this is more of that.

**Draft (Preface, after the ANSI/SPARC paragraph).**

> The word has an older philosophical sense, and it turns out not to be far
> away. For the early Wittgenstein, and for Langer after him, logical form is
> what a representation must share with what it represents in order to
> represent at all. The database designers and the logicians were naming the
> same layer from opposite ends: the part that must stay fixed so that
> everything else may change.

### 3. Rule-following as a third independent derivation of the load-bearing thesis — Chapters 5–6 *(high)*

**Where.** The end of Ch5 §IV announces an independent derivation "from
physics". Ch6 §IV then claims a convergence of two arguments that "share no
premises".

**Move.** The later Wittgenstein supplies a third line, from philosophy of
language, and it reaches the same criterion. The private-language remarks make
the point with unusual bluntness: where there is no check independent of the
checker, "whatever is going to seem right to me is right. And that only means
that here we can't talk about 'right'" (PI §258). An unenforced entry is in
exactly that condition. Nothing separates its being right from its seeming
right, because nothing that could disagree with it ever consults it. §202
completes the argument: "to *think* one is obeying a rule is not to obey a
rule". A representation that no process consumes is a rule no one is following,
however carefully it is written.

**Draft (for Ch5 §IV, before the handoff to physics).**

> Philosophy reached the same line by another road. Wittgenstein's argument
> against private rules is, in this book's vocabulary, an argument about
> non-load-bearing representations: where nothing independent can check an
> application, there is no difference between following the rule and merely
> seeming to — "and that only means that here we can't talk about 'right'." An
> entry that nothing consumes is not a correct rule awaiting use. It is not yet
> a rule at all.

**Why it helps.** Chapter 6's convergence claim grows from two lines to three,
and the third comes from the discipline closest to "meaning", which neither
the graveyard nor physics addresses head-on. It also supports the corollary's
harshest line ("documentation with a new name") with a reason, not only a
record.

**Risk.** Rule-following is among the most contested topics in the field. Keep
it to one paragraph, quote §202 and §258, cite Baker and Hacker (1984) or
McDowell (1984) for the reading, and don't adjudicate the scholarship.
Mentioning Kripke is optional, and if he appears it's as the reading the book
declines (see 4).

### 4. Resolve a tension in "Correlated Loss" with PI §142 and §50 — Chapter 10 §III *(high; this one fixes something)*

**Where.** Ch10 §III: "Admission is the act of resolution … so an entry that
stands in the assembly is the organization's meaning by definition, not a claim
awaiting grading. What an entry can do is age."

**Tension.** Read quickly, the sentence is Kripkean: whatever review admits is
correct by definition. That sits uneasily with Ch10 §II, where the outer loop
"verifies the assembly itself", and with the source note's warning that a
Kripkean reading turns into drift toward the newest row. The book's intended
distinction is between being wrong and having aged. It's sound, but it needs a
philosophical ground or it reads as a definitional escape hatch.

**Move.** The later Wittgenstein supplies that ground.

- An admitted entry works like a norm of description, a standard, rather than
  a report. The standard metre is neither one metre long nor not (PI §50). It's
  what lengths are measured against, and in the same way an entry is what
  conduct is checked against.
- A rule doesn't turn false when the world changes. It *loses its point*. Our
  procedures presuppose regularities, and "if rule became exception and
  exception rule" the language-game would lose its point (PI §142; the cheese on
  the scales that grows and shrinks). That is exactly "What an entry can do is
  age."
- Admission fixes what the entry *means*. It doesn't guarantee that the entry
  still has a point, and review can be shown wrong about that by evidence. That
  is the non-Kripkean reading from ground rule 3, and it is what the outer loop
  checks.

**Draft (replacing or following "What an entry can do is age").**

> An admitted entry is not a report about the domain but a standard for it —
> the thing conduct is measured against, as the metre was measured against the
> bar in Paris. Standards are not falsified. They lose their point, when the
> regularities they presuppose stop holding; a rule the world has left behind
> is not wrong but idle, and the outer loop exists to notice idleness before
> the gates enforce it.

**Why it helps.** It turns a definitional move into a principled one and shows
how the inner loop (conformance to the standard) and the outer loop (whether
the standard still has a point) differ in kind, not just in speed.

### 5. The dense substrate as the *record of use* — Chapter 9 §III *(medium-high)*

**Where.** Ch9 §III "Dense and Sparse": the model "knows what a refund is. What
it holds is the world's average refund."

**Move.** This is where the source note's latent-space reading belongs, in one
or two paragraphs. A foundation model is the distributional shadow of PI §43:
meaning learned from co-occurrence, which is the *record* of use rather than use
in a practice with training and correction (Harris 1954; Firth 1957; Bender and
Koller 2020). What it absorbs readily are names and relation types. What the
source note predicts it doesn't absorb from use are *invariants*, *policies*
and *authority*, the parts of a possibility space that a practice has to supply.
That sharpens the book's claim. The assembly isn't needed only because the
model holds the world's *average* refund. It's needed because some whole
components of an organization's meaning (constraints, policies, evaluations)
are not the kind of thing the record of use contains.

**Draft.**

> The substrate learned language the way the *Investigations* said meaning
> lives — from use — but it learned from the record of use, not from the
> practice: co-occurrence without correction. What such a record yields
> generously is vocabulary and relation, the names and their usual company.
> What it has not been shown to yield is the rest of a possibility space: the
> invariants that make a state legal, the policies that make a transition
> count, the authority that settles a dispute. That remainder is not a gap in
> the model's training. It is the part of meaning that only a practice can
> supply, and the assembly is where the practice writes it down.

**Optional Ch10 §IV fifth prediction**, hedged as the source note hedges it:
"Better substrates will close the gap on concepts and relations and will not
close it on constraints and policies." It could be falsified by a model that
reliably induces an organization's invariants from its instances. Include it
only if you want Part IV to carry a prediction about models rather than about
organizations. The source's prior rests on Vafa et al. (2024, 2025) and Cohen et
al. (2023), and the rig hasn't tested it.

**Risk.** This is the place where the book could start talking about latent
space for its own sake. Stop at the claim. Leave out probes, vindexes and PRH.

### 6. The binding as a *downward* projection — Chapter 9 §II, Chapter 8 §IV *(medium)*

**Where.** Ch9 §II "The Mirror Below", or the swap paragraph in Ch8 §IV.

**Move.** The book has projections going *up*: data models, agent configs,
reports. The source note's central engineering object, the binding (a
versioned map from assembly entries to places in one model, re-measured on every
swap), is a projection going *down*. It follows the book's rules for
projections exactly. It is derived from the assembly and never a source, it is
specific to one model, and it is disposable at every swap. The note's
recommendation ("a binding, not a brain") is the waist rule applied to the new
substrate: "nothing that bears the meaning may be entangled with a volatile
layer". It also gives the Ch8 swap an extra step: re-measure the bindings, and
let the ones that don't survive report *where* the new model fails the domain.

**Draft (one paragraph, Ch9 §II).**

> The mirror holds even where the substrate is most tempting. An assembly will
> want a footprint in the model it runs on — this entry corresponds to that
> region of the model's representation — and the footprint is useful. But it is
> a projection, downward: derived from one version of the assembly, specific to
> one model, re-measured at every swap and discarded with the model. The
> temptation to train the meaning *into* the substrate is the old entanglement
> in new clothes. Whatever is frozen into weights cannot be reviewed, contested,
> or rolled back, and the thing that must be revisable cannot be the thing that
> is frozen.

**Risk.** Low, provided it stays one paragraph. It is the least philosophical
suggestion and the most useful to Lasm readers.

### 7. Saying and showing at the formalization boundary — Chapter 10 §I *(medium)*

**Where.** Ch10 §I, first category (tacit, presentational): "The assembly should
not try."

**Move.** The boundary is older than 1942. Janik and Toulmin's reading of the
*Tractatus*' world-view ("the sphere of what can only be shown must be protected
from those who try to say it") is the ethical twin of the engineering rule in
this chapter. Langer's contribution was to decline Wittgenstein's
silence: what can't be said discursively can still be symbolized
presentationally. The assembly then takes a third position. It formalizes the
discursive and leaves the presentational to people, not because it is
ineffable but because enforcement would replace it with something else "wearing
its name". One sentence is enough, and Langer keeps the last word.

**Risk.** Don't bring the source note's say/show analogy for the
interpretability wall into the book. The note itself calls it only a partial
analogy, and it belongs to the rig.

### 8. Constraints as exclusions — Chapter 7 §I *(low-medium)*

**Where.** Ch7 §I, the definition of constraints ("what must hold for the domain
to be in a legal state").

**Move.** The first crack in the *Tractatus* was a constraint. Elementary facts
were meant to be independent (2.061), but colour exclusion (6.3751) showed that
some facts rule others out, and in "Some Remarks on Logical Form" (1929)
Wittgenstein gave up independence. An organization's possibility space is made
largely of exclusions: exactly one destination, no refund above its payment, a
suspended account places no orders. That's a one-sentence gloss on why
constraints are the component that turns a list of concepts into a *space*. It
also shows why a Lasm isn't a Tractarian logical space (suggestion 9).

### 9. A fence: "not a logical space" — Chapter 7 §III *(low-medium)*

**Where.** Ch7 §III "What a Lasm Is Not". That section is already a list of
fences, so this one fits.

**Move.** Once Chapter 1 has brought in the *Tractatus*, the definition "an
organization's possibility space made explicit" invites the reading "a logical
space". Close that off. A Tractarian logical space is total, fixed, and the
same for everyone. An assembly is scoped to one organization, revised through
reviewed diffs, and made largely of material exclusions. In later-Wittgenstein
terms it is closer to a calculus set up as an object of comparison and a norm
(PI §130–131) than to a picture of the world's form. This also answers the
source note's own finding that "possibility space" is the right name.

**Draft.**

> Nor is it a logical space in the *Tractatus*'s sense — total, fixed, the same
> for every speaker. An assembly is one organization's space, drawn where its
> business needs distinctions, revised by reviewed diff, and held together
> largely by what it excludes. It is less a picture of the world's form than a
> measuring rod the organization has agreed to hold its conduct against.

## Considered and set aside

- **6.43, "the will alters only the limits of the world".** It's apt for
  Nuveris directives, but it has no counterpart in the book and would bring in
  confidential material by implication.
- **"Ethics is taught by examples" as a gloss on evaluations.** The known-good
  and known-bad episodes do resemble teaching by example. But the source note
  itself says the analogy concerns how norms are transmitted, not the ethical
  will, and making that precise would cost a paragraph the book doesn't need.
  Chapter 8's Toyoda loom already does the work more concretely.
- **The gate as Frege's judgement-stroke restored.** The idea is to separate
  forming a claim from asserting it. It's elegant, but it's a third
  philosopher in one move, and Ch8 §I's "exam versus lock" already makes the
  point.
- **The "three departures" frame** (provenance 5.542, attestation 4.442,
  exclusion 6.3751). It's a strong organizing idea for the Lasm engineering
  notes. In the book only exclusion lands (suggestion 8). Provenance is already
  covered by Ch9 §IV's review thread ("over whose objection"), and attestation
  is Nuveris-specific.
- **PRH and cross-model alignment.** It belongs to the rig. At most it gets a
  clause inside suggestion 6: similarity structure may carry across models,
  addresses never do.

## Suggested order of work

Edits 1–4 form one connected thread and should land together. 1 introduces the
figure, 2 names Langer's debt and updates the Preface, 3 uses him in the
central argument, and 4 uses him to repair Ch10 §III. Those alone make
Wittgenstein a structural presence rather than a decoration. After that, 5 and
6 bring the latent-space material in at the one place the book already
discusses models. 7–9 are optional polish.

Together these touch roughly six chapters, but each edit is a paragraph or two
rather than a rewrite. Taken as a planned change to the manuscript, the set is
the right size for one ReffySpec change (`add-wittgenstein-thread`, say), if you
want the edits tracked as tasks.

## Open questions for the author

- **How much Wittgenstein?** Here he is a *thread*: one section in Chapter 1
  and recurring glosses after it. The alternative is a single *station* in the
  relay, with Chapter 1 only. With a single station, suggestions 3 and 4 lose
  their footing, and 4 is the one that repairs something.
- **Does the Preface change?** Suggestion 2 argues yes. It's your preface, and
  its dismissal of the philosophical lineage may be deliberate.
- **Which translation?** The two PI translations word §202 and §258
  differently, and the drafts above lean on the Anscombe phrasing.
- **Does Langer's debt check out?** Suggestion 2 depends on the PNK passages.
  If they're weaker than remembered, soften the claim to "in the tradition of
  the *Tractatus*".
