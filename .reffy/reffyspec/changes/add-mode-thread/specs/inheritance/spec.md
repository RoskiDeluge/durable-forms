## ADDED Requirements
### Requirement: Spinoza appears as the source of Deleuze's immanence
Chapter 1 §IV SHALL add, after the paragraph on the virtual and the actual and before "We do not follow Deleuze all the way down", no more than two paragraphs introducing Spinoza as the philosopher from whom Deleuze's overturning of Platonism takes its immanence. They SHALL state that substance is known under two attributes, extension and thought, and that a *mode* is a particular thing expressed under both at once, one thing seen two ways. They SHALL give *Ethics* II, proposition 7 (the order and connection of ideas is the same as the order and connection of things). They SHALL say that Spinoza builds no wall between things as they appear and things as they are, and that he is not a mechanist, because neither attribute reduces to the other. They SHALL draw the consequence for the book: an act and its meaning can be kept as one record rather than a fact plus an annotation. They SHALL bring in Kant by name as Spinoza's counterweight: the wall between appearances and things in themselves, and the transcendental as the conditions of possible experience. They SHALL show that the book's line through the *Tractatus* and Langer runs through Kant's wall, and SHALL name the Spinoza–Kant tension without resolving it. They SHALL NOT import Spinoza's ethics, politics or *conatus*.

#### Scenario: Reader follows the relay
- **WHEN** a reader moves from Langer to Deleuze in Chapter 1
- **THEN** Spinoza arrives as Deleuze's ancestor, not as a new protagonist
- **AND** the word *mode* is defined in its Spinozist sense before Chapter 8 uses it

#### Scenario: Transcendental tension
- **WHEN** a reader compares the Spinoza passage with the *Tractatus* and Langer sections
- **THEN** the disagreement is named as a disagreement between Spinoza and Kant
- **AND** the book says what it takes from each: from Spinoza, what a kept record is; from the transcendental strand, what any frame cannot hold
