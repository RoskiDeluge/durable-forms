## ADDED Requirements
### Requirement: The constitution has a hinge floor and an amendment clause
Chapter 10 §I SHALL add, after the third category, a coda of no more than two paragraphs. It SHALL say that some of what the archaeology surfaces never lands on the adjudication table because it is what makes the table a table: commitments that define what load means for the organization. It SHALL name these hinges, after *On Certainty* §§341–343, and SHALL present the later Wittgenstein's account on which the regress of justification ends in action, not in a further proposition. It SHALL extend the constitution figure already in §I with an amendment clause: hinges are declared, visible, chosen rather than inherited, kept outside the ordinary diff queue, and amendable only by a slower and costlier process, because the meta-level must be slow relative to the object level. It SHALL make clear that hinges are not entries and are not unrevisable. If it quotes "my spade is turned", it SHALL cite PI §217.

#### Scenario: Reader reaches the end of the formalization boundary
- **WHEN** a reader reads what the archaeology should decline
- **THEN** they also meet the floor beneath the assembly, declared and priced
- **AND** the diff discipline of Chapter 9 §IV is not contradicted: hinges change by a slower process, not never

#### Scenario: Kodak
- **WHEN** the coda illustrates the price of a truncated tower
- **THEN** it does so without the Kodak example unless the author has pinned a source

### Requirement: The commitment section no longer denies structural blindness
Chapter 10 §II SHALL revise the sentence "it can never be as blind as the artifacts it replaced, because blindness — divergence that no consequence reaches — is the specific condition the scoping rule was written to exclude" so that it claims only that the assembly cannot be blind to what it checks, and SHALL point forward to the closed-world section for what reaches no gate. The change SHALL be no more than one sentence.

#### Scenario: Reader compares §II and the closed-world section
- **WHEN** a reader reads §II's claim about blindness and then the closed-world section
- **THEN** the two do not contradict each other
- **AND** "performance" remains §II's live failure mode

### Requirement: The closed world is the third exposure
Chapter 10 SHALL add a section titled "The Closed World", placed between "Correlated Loss" and "The Predictions", of no more than five paragraphs. "The Predictions" and "What Is Kept" SHALL renumber accordingly. The section SHALL:
- name the closed-world assumption (treating "not representable here" as "not real") as the formal signature of hubris, and say that most systems make it by default, not by decision;
- say that the scoping rule excludes entries nothing checks but cannot exclude reality that reaches no gate, and that the guarantee of Chapter 8 §II covers only what arrives at a gate;
- define the residue as what the gates reject or cannot parse, and require that it stay the least typed part of the system, because structuring it annexes it into the frame and the true residue moves outside again (the catch-all category that acquires subcategories and resets the gauge);
- say what may be typed: the residue's dynamics (volume, recurrence, clustering, which gate it arrived at), measured by the instruments of Chapter 8 §III;
- state that zero residue is the alarm condition, and that clustering residue is reality proposing a category, to be heard at the diff queue;
- state that the assembly can detect accumulation but cannot judge what it means, so the obligation that someone with authority reads the residue is a commitment of §II's kind;
- state explicitly that the residue is not a seventh component.
It SHALL NOT cite Peirce, Lakatos, Ashby, Gödel or Turing.

#### Scenario: Reader arrives at the exposures
- **WHEN** a reader reads Chapter 10's exposures in order
- **THEN** they meet three: the commitment, correlated loss, and the closed world
- **AND** the third corrects the book's earlier claim rather than contradicting it

#### Scenario: Reader checks the anatomy
- **WHEN** a reader compares the closed-world section with Chapter 7
- **THEN** Chapter 7 still lists six components and four properties
- **AND** the residue is presented as an obligation on the gates and the organization

#### Scenario: Skeptic starts at Chapter 10
- **WHEN** a skeptic reads the closed-world section first
- **THEN** they find the limit of the guarantee stated before the predictions, in the book's own voice
