## ADDED Requirements
### Requirement: Rule-following as an independent derivation of the load-bearing thesis
Chapter 5 SHALL include, at the end of §IV, a paragraph that derives the load-bearing criterion from Wittgenstein's rule-following remarks. Where no check exists independently of the checker, there is no difference between being right and seeming right (PI §258). Thinking one is obeying a rule is not obeying it (PI §202). The paragraph SHALL quote Anscombe's translation, SHALL take the reading on which a practice gives a rule its content without making whatever it approves correct (Baker and Hacker, or McDowell), and SHALL be no longer than two paragraphs. The chapter's handoff to physics SHALL no longer imply that physics is the only independent derivation.

#### Scenario: Reader finishes Chapter 5
- **WHEN** a reader reaches the end of Chapter 5 §IV
- **THEN** they meet an argument from philosophy of language that an entry nothing consumes is not yet a rule
- **AND** the transition to Chapter 6 still introduces physics as a further independent line

#### Scenario: Kripke is not adopted
- **WHEN** the paragraph characterizes what practice does for a rule
- **THEN** it does not say that community agreement makes an application correct

### Requirement: Chapter 6 convergence counts three lines of argument
Chapter 6 §IV SHALL describe the load-bearing criterion as reached by three independent lines: the graveyard, constructor theory, and the rule-following argument. It SHALL keep its caveat that convergence is not proof.

#### Scenario: Reader reaches the convergence claim
- **WHEN** a reader reads Chapter 6 §IV's statement of convergence
- **THEN** it names three lines of argument that share no premises
- **AND** it still says convergence of independent arguments is not proof
