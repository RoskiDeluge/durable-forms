## ADDED Requirements
### Requirement: Three altitudes hold the Spinoza–Kant question
Chapter 10 §VI SHALL add, after the retention-era paragraph and before Langer's paragraph, no more than one paragraph setting out three altitudes. The first is the record (Spinoza): one order under two descriptions, with the mode as its unit. The second is the participants (Leibniz): many points of view coordinated by a harmony that is kept. The third is the frame (Kant, the later Wittgenstein, Langer): the conditions under which anything can appear in the assembly, and the judgment that draws and amends them. It SHALL place the five questions at the frame altitude, as the form any participant must take in order to appear. It SHALL name the faculty that fills them after Kant's *Critique of Judgment* (bringing particulars under concepts) and SHALL point back to §IV's "no frame draws itself". It SHALL say plainly that Spinoza and Leibniz disagreed about one substance or many, and that the book uses each at a different altitude. It SHALL NOT claim that Spinoza and Kant are reconciled, only that the assembly needs both, at different heights. Langer's paragraph SHALL remain last and unchanged.

#### Scenario: Reader reaches "What Is Kept"
- **WHEN** a reader reaches the end of the book
- **THEN** Spinoza, Leibniz and the transcendental line each speak for one altitude
- **AND** the Spinoza–Kant question is held, not settled
- **AND** Langer has the last word

#### Scenario: Careful reader checks the rivalry
- **WHEN** a reader who knows that Spinoza and Leibniz disagreed reads the three altitudes
- **THEN** the book names the dispute and says it uses them as levels
