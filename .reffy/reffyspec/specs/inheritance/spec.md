## Requirements

### Requirement: Wittgenstein appears in the relay of corrections
Chapter 1 SHALL include a section, placed between the Plato/Aristotle section and the Langer section, that presents Wittgenstein as the thinker who ran the relay within a single career. It SHALL present the *Tractatus* as a total, fixed logical space whose objects are unalterable (TLP 2.0271, 3.42) and which nothing maintains, i.e. Plato's physics again. It SHALL present the *Investigations* as the correction, with meaning in use (PI §43) and rules as practices (PI §202). The section SHALL be no longer than three paragraphs and SHALL NOT go into the internals of the picture theory.

#### Scenario: Reader follows the relay
- **WHEN** a reader reads Chapter 1 from Plato to Langer
- **THEN** they meet Wittgenstein between them, as both a repetition of the Platonic physics and its correction
- **AND** the chapter's opening list of the relay and §VI "What Remains" both reflect his place in it

#### Scenario: Janik and Toulmin connection
- **WHEN** the section describes the *Tractatus*' eventual status
- **THEN** it may use Janik and Toulmin's "a kind of Platonic myth" to link it to the chapter's Platonic frame, cited to *Wittgenstein's Vienna*

### Requirement: Langer's logical form is credited to the Tractatus
Chapter 1's Langer section SHALL state that her notion of logical form, what a symbol shares with what it symbolizes, comes from the *Tractatus* (TLP 2.18). It SHALL state this without hedging, as settled by the author against *Philosophy in a New Key*.

#### Scenario: Reader reaches Langer's logical form
- **WHEN** a reader reads the sentence introducing Langer's "logical form"
- **THEN** the *Tractatus* is named as its source
- **AND** Langer remains the section's subject

### Requirement: Spinoza appears as the source of Deleuze's immanence
Chapter 1 §IV SHALL add, after the paragraph on the virtual and the actual and before "We do not follow Deleuze all the way down", no more than two paragraphs introducing Spinoza as the philosopher from whom Deleuze's overturning of Platonism takes its immanence. They SHALL state that substance is known under two attributes, extension and thought, and that a *mode* is a particular thing expressed under both at once, one thing seen two ways. They SHALL give *Ethics* II, proposition 7 (the order and connection of ideas is the same as the order and connection of things). They SHALL say that Spinoza builds no wall between things as they appear and things as they are, and that he is not a mechanist, because neither attribute reduces to the other. They SHALL draw the consequence for the book: an act and its meaning can be kept as one record rather than a fact plus an annotation. They SHALL bring in Kant by name as Spinoza's counterweight: the wall between appearances and things in themselves, and the transcendental as the conditions of possible experience. They SHALL show that the book's line through the *Tractatus* and Langer runs through Kant's wall, and SHALL name the Spinoza–Kant tension without resolving it. They SHALL NOT import Spinoza's ethics, politics or *conatus*.

#### Scenario: Reader follows the relay
- **WHEN** a reader moves from Langer to Deleuze in Chapter 1
- **THEN** Spinoza arrives as Deleuze's ancestor, not as a new protagonist
- **AND** the word *mode* is defined in its Spinozist sense before Chapter 8 uses it

#### Scenario: Transcendental tension
- **WHEN** a reader compares the Spinoza passage with the *Tractatus* and Langer sections
- **THEN** the disagreement is named as a disagreement between Spinoza and Kant
- **AND** the book says what it takes from each: from Spinoza, what a kept record is; from the transcendental strand, what any frame cannot hold

### Requirement: Leibniz appears between Spinoza and Kant
Chapter 1 §IV SHALL add, between the Spinoza paragraph and the Kant paragraph, no more than one paragraph introducing Leibniz as the figure who stands between them historically and conceptually. It SHALL say that Leibniz visited Spinoza in The Hague in 1676 and read parts of the *Ethics* in manuscript, and that the *Monadology* (1714) answers Spinoza with infinitely many simple substances, monads, rather than one. It SHALL give three claims: monads are windowless (§7), each mirrors the whole from its own point of view (§56), and they agree through a pre-established harmony (§78), not through interaction. It SHALL mark the harmony's guarantee by God as the point where the book will part from Leibniz, and it SHALL plant the monad and the harmony for Chapter 8. It SHALL NOT import Leibniz's theodicy, his calculus, or the best of all possible worlds.

#### Scenario: Reader follows the relay
- **WHEN** a reader moves from the Spinoza paragraph to the Kant paragraph
- **THEN** Leibniz arrives as the reaction to Spinoza that Kant would in turn react against
- **AND** the word *monad* is defined in its Leibnizian sense before Chapter 8 uses it

### Requirement: The Kant passage answers Leibniz and forecasts three altitudes
Chapter 1 §IV's Kant paragraph SHALL present Kant's wall as a reaction to the Leibniz–Wolff school as well as to Spinoza. It SHALL close with no more than two sentences forecasting three altitudes at which the book holds the question it declines to settle: the record, the participants, and the frame. It SHALL point to Chapter 10 for them. It SHALL keep the Spinoza–Kant tension named and unresolved, and SHALL keep what the book takes from each side.

#### Scenario: Reader reaches the end of the Kant paragraph
- **WHEN** a reader finishes Chapter 1 §IV's account of Kant
- **THEN** the open question is still open
- **AND** the reader has been told that Chapter 10 gives each tradition its own altitude
